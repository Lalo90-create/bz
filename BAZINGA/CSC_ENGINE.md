# Bazinga Lab — CSC Commercial Engine

## Objective
Turn CSC/club relationships into lawful, professional and repeatable revenue.

## Priority order
1. Warm contacts / existing relationships
2. Clubs in Mazarrón, Cartagena, Murcia and La Manga
3. Clubs with visible operational, training, menu, CRM or event needs
4. Partners who can introduce multiple clubs

## Qualification
For each club, JARVIS should determine:
- Is the club active and reachable?
- Do we know someone inside or have a warm path?
- What operational problem is visible or likely?
- Which Bazinga offer fits?
- What is the smallest paid first step?
- Can the service become recurring?
- Can a successful case produce referrals?

## Opportunity score
Use the global Money Engine, plus these CSC signals:
- warm relationship;
- clear decision-maker;
- visible pain point;
- fast-to-deliver service;
- recurring potential;
- referral potential.

## Outreach sequence
Do not mass-spam.

Preferred sequence:
1. Research the club
2. Identify one specific reason to contact
3. Choose ONE entry offer
4. Prepare a short personalized message
5. Lautaro approves before sending
6. Follow up
7. Move the lead through CRM

## Entry-offer logic
Prefer easy-to-understand offers first:
- mini audit;
- menu review;
- staff training session;
- event/workshop pilot;
- CRM/process review.

Avoid leading with a huge consulting package before trust exists.

## Recurring conversion
After a successful entry service, evaluate:
- monthly menu support;
- recurring staff training;
- quarterly operational review;
- event calendar;
- CRM/process support;
- partner/vendor coordination.

## Weekly CSC sprint
A standard sprint should target:
- 10 qualified CSC/club leads;
- 5 personalized outreach drafts;
- 3 follow-ups;
- 1–2 meetings or concrete conversations as the initial goal;
- one tested offer;
- one learning captured into the playbook.

These are operational starting targets, not guaranteed outcomes. Adjust them using real conversion data.
